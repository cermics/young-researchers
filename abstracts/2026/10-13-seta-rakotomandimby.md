**Speaker:** Seta Rakotomandimby  
**Date:** Tuesday 13 October 2026

## Abstract

This thesis consists of two parts.

In the first part, we tackle sparse optimization based on previous work on Capra-convexity. Capra-convexity is defined from a generalization of the Legendre-Fenchel transform in which the scalar product is replaced by the ratio between the scalar product and a norm called a source norm. This study is motivated by applications in compressed sensing and model selection in supervised learning, where the pseudonorm ℓ₀ is ubiquitous. We characterize Capra-convex sets and give sufficient conditions for a conic hull to be Capra-convex. We also prove that Capra-convexity extends (closed) spherical convexity. In parallel, we design a convergent Capra-cutting-plane method for constrained minimization of ℓ₀. We also outline approximations of the pseudonorm ℓ₀ that could lead to an efficient Capra-cutting-plane method for sparse minimization.

In the second part, we study a new family of losses that we call representation losses. We extend the recent framework of Fenchel-Young losses to an axiomatic framework of loss functions. This allows us to introduce the theory of the representation of maximally monotone operators into supervised learning. Such losses include Fenchel-Young losses and Fitzpatrick losses. We study Fitzpatrick losses through numerical experiments and we display and prove a parameterized Fenchel-Young property. Losses satisfying such a property are called Fenchel-Young sharpened losses. Finally, we study the conditions under which a Fenchel-Young sharpened loss is a representation loss and, conversely, under which a representation loss is a Fenchel-Young sharpened loss.
